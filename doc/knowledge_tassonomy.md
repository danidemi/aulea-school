## 1. The Epistemological Framework (Gilbert Ryle & Aristotle)

Philosopher Gilbert Ryle (*The Concept of Mind*, 1949) famously formalized the distinction you mentioned:

* **Propositional Knowledge (*Knowing-that*):** Facts, assertions, and theoretical information (e.g., *knowing that water boils at 100°C*).
* **Procedural Knowledge (*Knowing-how*):** The capacity to execute an action, skill, or process successfully (e.g., *knowing how to swim*).

This builds on **Aristotle’s classical tri-part distinction**:

1. ***Episteme***: Scientific/theoretical knowledge of universal truths.
2. ***Techne***: Craftsmanship or practical skill oriented toward creation.
3. ***Phronesis***: Practical wisdom or ethical judgment—knowing *how to live well* and make context-appropriate decisions.

## 2. The Cognitive Psychology Framework

Cognitive science maps knowledge based on how the human brain processes, stores, and retrieves information.

* **Declarative Knowledge ("Knowing What"):**
* *Semantic Memory:* General facts, concepts, and principles independent of personal experience.
* *Episodic Memory:* Knowledge tied to specific events or personal experiences.


* **Procedural Knowledge ("Knowing How"):**
* Automated motor skills, cognitive routines, and algorithmic steps. Often operates subconsciously (e.g., touch-typing).


* **Conditional Knowledge ("Knowing When and Why"):**
* Strategic and contextual knowledge. It dictates under what circumstances declarative and procedural knowledge should be deployed.



---

## 3. The Knowledge Management Spectrum (Polanyi & Nonaka)

Introduced by Michael Polanyi and popularized by Ikujiro Nonaka, this view categorizes knowledge by **how easily it can be articulated and transferred**:

> *"We can know more than we can tell."* — Michael Polanyi

* **Explicit Knowledge:** Codified, structured knowledge easily recorded, stored, and shared via language, books, or code (maps directly to *Declarative / Knowing-that*).
* **Tacit Knowledge:** Intuitive, context-bound, personal knowledge gained through experience. It is notoriously difficult to write down (maps directly to *Procedural / Knowing-how*).
